🤖 Changpeng Zhao after the Binance hack: "AI has made hacking much easier"

Changpeng Zhao (CZ), co-founder of Binance, speaking to Brian Sozzi of Yahoo Finance after the theft of about $6.3 billion from the exchange, did not talk only about one hack. His warning was for the entire financial system.

🔹 What is his main point?
• According to him, AI has made hacking much easier, but it has not yet made defence equally easy.
• The reason is that these tools can analyse code and look for weaknesses and bugs, and do so on a very wide scale.
• He says this risk is not limited to crypto; it covers every crypto platform, wallet, bank and financial institution.
• His prediction is that, with the rapid progress of AI, attacks of this kind will increase.
• His solution: "We must use AI too." According to him, the entire global financial system has to catch up in AI-powered defence.
• And one line we would all do well to remember: "There is no such thing as 100% security."

🔹 What did he say about Binance itself?
He says withdrawals have resumed and systems are back to normal, all user funds have been covered and users have not lost anything. He also said he had not expected a hack of this size.

⚠️ Three points for reading these remarks correctly
• There is no sign that this hack was carried out with AI. According to reports, the attackers did not break any private keys; they reached the automated transaction processes through an employee's laptop.
• The FBI and blockchain analytics firms have attributed the attack to a group linked to North Korea; North Korea has denied it.
• CZ no longer runs Binance, but he is a shareholder. So his statements about full coverage of losses should be read as the word of one party to the story.

🔹 What do other experts say?
Many agree with the core concern, but say defenders are using the same tools. Gonçalo Magalhães of security firm Immunefi told The National: AI has made finding weaknesses cheap, but fixing them still moves at human speed.

🔍 Ditoman's view
In our view, the most important point is exactly this gap: finding a hole has become faster, but closing it has not. For us as users, the meaning is simple:
• Let's not assume any platform is 100% safe, not even the biggest ones.
• Let's not keep all our assets in one place, and not leave on an exchange what we don't need day to day.
• Let's be more suspicious of unexpected emails, links and messages; a single infected laptop can be where the story starts.
