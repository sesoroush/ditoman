🖥 AI in a small box: AMD says you don't always need to be connected to the cloud

AMD CEO Lisa Su held up a small computer on stage and said large AI models can run on the device itself, without a cloud server. The device is called the Ryzen AI Halo, and it is now on sale in the US.

⚙️ What does it have?
🔹 A Ryzen AI Max+ 395 chip with 16 cores and an NPU rated at 50 TOPS
🔹 128 GB of unified memory shared by the CPU and graphics; this is what lets large models fit in it
🔹 According to AMD, it can run models of up to about 200 billion parameters (depending on the type of compression)
🔹 Runs Windows and Linux
🔹 Price: $3,999; its direct rival, the Nvidia DGX Spark, costs about $4,679

✅ Why is it interesting?
Your data stays on your own device, you don't pay per request, and there is no usage cap. For companies, researchers and developers who work with sensitive information, this means more control.

⚠️ But the headline "it kills the $200 subscription" is an exaggeration
▫️ The models that run on this device are open-source (such as GPT-OSS and Qwen); they are usually not on par with the best cloud models
▫️ The price is about $4,000, equal to about 20 months of a $200 subscription, and other companies' machines with the same chip usually cost $2,400 to $4,000
▫️ The 4 to 14 percent edge over the DGX Spark and "3 times faster than the RTX 5080" are AMD's own claims, for specific models and settings; independent testing is still needed
▫️ With very large models, text generation speed is slow

🔍 Ditoman's view
This news is not about replacing the cloud; it is a sign of a shift: AI is moving from "only on big companies' servers" to "on your own desk". It is not cheap yet, but the direction is clear: cheaper devices, stronger open-source models, and a choice between the cloud and a personal device.
