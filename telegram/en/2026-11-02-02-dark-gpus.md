⚡️ Dark chips: billions of dollars of AI, with no power!

Imagine you have bought billions of dollars' worth of the world's most advanced AI chips, but you still do not have enough electricity to switch some of them on!

According to Business Insider, this is exactly the problem Amazon is facing.

🔌 What is the Dark GPUs story?

According to the report, which is based on Amazon's internal emails, the company's cloud division (AWS) planned to receive about $5 billion of advanced NVIDIA GB300 equipment in August, and after that about $3 billion of further equipment each month.

But there is a problem: some of Amazon's data centres do not yet have enough power capacity to run all the equipment they receive.

As a result, part of this equipment is being kept in warehouses for now and has not yet entered the processing cycle.

Processors that are switched off or sitting unused like this are called "Dark GPUs".

💰 Billions of dollars of capital, waiting for power!

Amazon has forecast about $220 billion of capital expenditure for 2026.

At the same time, the company's free cash flow for the twelve months to September reached minus $42.4 billion, while revenue at its AWS cloud division grew 39% in the third quarter.

These numbers show that even with demand for AI growing fast, building its infrastructure requires very heavy investment.

Amazon has stressed, however, that storing equipment temporarily before installation is a normal part of planning data-centre expansion and does not mean it has no plans to use it.

⚡️ The bigger problem: is there enough power?

The issue is not just Amazon.

As AI spreads, tech companies need more processors, data centres, cooling systems and, above all, electricity.

Morgan Stanley estimates that US data-centre expansion could face a power capacity shortfall of 32 gigawatts by 2028, equal to about 34% of projected demand.

In other words, the pace of investment in AI may outrun the pace of energy infrastructure development.

🔍 Ditoman's view | Why does it matter?

The AI race is no longer just about building the fastest and most powerful chips.

Electricity, power transmission grids, cooling systems and the time it takes to prepare data centres have become decisive factors in this race.

Every day of delay in switching on expensive equipment can mean delayed revenue, higher capital costs and even a fall in the economic value of chips compared with newer generations.

The important point, though, is that it is still unclear how much of Amazon's equipment is actually sitting unused. The billion-dollar figures in the report are the value of equipment scheduled for delivery, not a confirmed value of switched-off chips.

💡 An interesting question:

Will the biggest obstacle to AI growth in the coming years be a shortage of chips, or a shortage of electricity to switch them on?
