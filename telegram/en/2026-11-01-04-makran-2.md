🇮🇳 6. India: a key partner on the Chabahar route
In 2024 India and Iran signed a ten-year contract to equip and operate the Shahid Beheshti terminal at Chabahar.
Under this contract India committed $120 million to equipping the port.
Chabahar matters to India because it offers a route to Afghanistan and Central Asia without having to cross Pakistan by land.
Even so, sanctions and banking restrictions remain obstacles to developing this route.

🇵🇰 7. Pakistan and Gwadar: two ports in one strategic region
On the other side of the Makran coast, Pakistan's port of Gwadar has been developed with Chinese participation.
Chabahar and Gwadar are both trying to connect Indian Ocean trade to the inland markets of Asia.
This geographic closeness can be a source of competition for cargo and investment, and also an opportunity for regional cooperation.

🌍 8. Can Chabahar compete with the UAE's ports?
One of Makran's biggest opportunities is building logistics hubs, warehousing, packaging, processing and re-export of goods.
Dubai's Jebel Ali port has for years played a major role in trade and re-distribution of goods in the region.
Developing Chabahar could help Iran build part of these services at home, especially for trade with India, Afghanistan and Central Asia.
But competing with big hubs like Jebel Ali does not depend only on berth capacity. Banking services, insurance, fast customs clearance, regular shipping lines and stable regulations are also essential.

🦐 9. The maritime economy: from shrimp farming to export earnings
Alongside the infrastructure projects, southern Iran's marine industries also have significant export potential.
In the first half of 1405 (March to September 2026), exports of farmed shrimp from Bushehr to Russia reached about 6,474 tonnes, worth nearly $22 million.
That is growth of 152 percent in weight and 382 percent in value compared with the same period of the previous year.
If cold storage, processing industries and export transport on the southern coast expand, new opportunities will open up for seafood producers.

🔍 Ditoman's view | What is the real story?
The transformation of Makran is not just about building a port or a railway; it is the story of connecting several parts of the economy to one another.
A port without a railway, a railway without industry, and industry without a city and workforce cannot unlock their full economic potential.
If this chain is completed, Iran could have more diverse routes for imports and exports, reduce its geographic dependence on the Strait of Hormuz and win a larger share of the region's transit trade.

⚠️ But one important reality: many of these projects are not yet finished. Implementation delays, sanctions, financial constraints and competition from regional ports remain serious challenges.

The key question is: can Makran grow from a collection of construction projects into a real hub of trade, industry and production?
