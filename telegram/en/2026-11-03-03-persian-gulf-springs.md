🌊 The hidden springs of the Persian Gulf: the Iranian half of this map is still blank

Imagine the Persian Gulf was not a sea.
During the ice ages, the floor of this shallow gulf became almost entirely dry land. A river formed by the joining of the Tigris, the Euphrates and the Karun flowed through it and reached the Gulf of Oman through the Strait of Hormuz.

🔎 What does the new research say?
In Tir this year (June–July 2026), researchers from the University of the Algarve in Portugal, the University of Vienna and the National Museum of Qatar published a model suggesting that groundwater pressure could have brought fresh water to the surface on this dry land. Their upper estimate is about 36,000 square kilometres. However, this figure has an uncertainty of up to about tenfold, and not all of this area had water.

🗺 But the map is only half done
The model covers only the Arabian half of the Gulf and leaves Iran's coastline for separate data.

🇮🇷 And this is where Iranian researchers come in
🔹 Shahid Beheshti University: in 74 marine profiles, signs of fresher water and more oxygen were seen near the seabed, especially next to three fault zones.
🔹 The Bushehr coast: using satellite thermal images, researchers searched for the temperature pattern of six Bahraini springs at Asaluyeh, Nayband, Bandar Deyr, Genaveh and Deylam.
🔹 Another study in Bushehr: about 22,317 hectares of possible groundwater discharge zones.
🔹 Hormozgan: from Bandar Moqam to Kong, the likelihood of such discharge has been estimated as very high. If the water quality is suitable, it could also be a water source.

🏺 The story is not new
In Bahrain, divers used to bring fresh water up from the seabed in waterskins. About 1,200 years ago, an artificial island was even built around one of these springs. According to an old account, the city of Hormuz also brought part of its drinking water from the springs around Bahrain.

⚠️ Where should we be careful?
The foreign model is about the ice age and the Arabian side, and has not yet been peer-reviewed. The Iranian studies are about recent times and the Iranian side, and mostly use satellite and marine survey data. These are signs, not a definitive discovery, and the amount and quality of the water still need to be measured.
