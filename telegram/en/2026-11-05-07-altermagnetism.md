🧲 After 90 years, a third type of magnetism is officially confirmed

From 1933 until today, physics recognised only two types of magnetic order: ferromagnetism (an ordinary magnet; electron spins aligned in the same direction, a strong field) and antiferromagnetism (spins alternating in opposite directions; zero net field). Now a team at the US SLAC laboratory has published conclusive evidence of a third type in the journal Science: altermagnetism.

🔬 What is the difference?
It looks like antiferromagnetism (zero net field), but the crystal structure around each spin is rotated relative to its neighbour. This subtle rotation means the material, despite having zero field, produces a "spin current", a property previously expected only from ordinary magnets. In other words, the stability of antiferromagnets plus the readability of ferromagnets, at the same time.

🧪 How was it proven?
In manganese telluride (MnTe), which for decades was considered an antiferromagnet, polarised X-rays at the ESRF synchrotron in France revealed "chirality" (handedness, like the difference between a left and a right hand), a signature that ordinary antiferromagnets do not have. The experiment was carried out in 2024 and repeated in 2025.

💾 What is it good for?
Next-generation magnetic memory: bits that cannot be erased by an ordinary magnet but can be read and written quickly, with switching speeds up to a thousand times faster than today's ferromagnets and higher density. For data centres and AI chips that means lower power consumption; for sensors and quantum devices it means resistance to magnetic noise.

🏭 Who will feel it first?
Memory and chip makers (Samsung, Micron, SK hynix, Intel, TSMC), MRAM companies such as Everspin, and later data centre and cloud operators.

⏳ When will it become real?
1 to 3 years of lab work (controlling this state with light and electric current, finding materials compatible with silicon); 3 to 7 years to early prototypes; 7 to 15 years to real products, if a manufacturing path works out. This is a confirmed fundamental discovery, not a product on the way.
