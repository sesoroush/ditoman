🧠 A weight-loss drug, electronic this time?

Today millions of people use injections such as Wegovy and Zepbound to curb their appetite. Now a Chinese company says a headband might one day do the same job: no drugs, no surgery.

📍 What happened?
Nicks He, co-founder of BrainCo, said at the Bloomberg Tech event in London on 3 November that the company is working on a wearable device that reduces appetite by stimulating the brain. He called it an "electronic version of GLP-1".
▫️ Goal: reach the market in 2 to 3 years
▫️ China first, then possibly the US
▫️ Probably two versions: medical and consumer

🏢 Who is BrainCo?
▫️ Founded in 2015 by Bicheng Han, a PhD student at Harvard's Center for Brain Science, at the Harvard Innovation Labs; headquartered in Hangzhou, China, since 2018
▫️ A rival to Elon Musk's Neuralink, with one important difference: Neuralink implants a chip in the brain, while BrainCo works on external, non-surgical devices
▫️ It already sells brain-computer interface headbands for autism, ADHD and insomnia, a prosthetic hand controlled by the body's signals, and robotic hands for the humanoid robot industry
▫️ In January 2026 it raised about 2 billion yuan (about $280 million) and, according to Bloomberg, has filed confidentially for a share listing in Hong Kong

⚙️ How might it work?
GLP-1 drugs mimic a hormone that sends the brain the message "I'm full". BrainCo's idea is to deliver that same message to the brain with weak electrical currents instead of a drug.
The science behind it is not zero: a review in Frontiers in Neuroscience (2018) found that non-invasive stimulation of the front part of the brain (tDCS and rTMS) reduced food cravings in small studies; but the effect on how much people eat and on weight is still inconsistent, and large trials are needed.

❓ What is still unknown?
The target area in the brain, clinical data, price and the regulatory path. No results from human testing of this device have been published so far.

🔮 If it works, what could it do?
▫️ A drug-free option for people who cannot tolerate the side effects or cost of the injections
▫️ A tool you can switch off, with no drug staying in the body
▫️ The next step for "health wearables": from a watch that counts your heartbeat to a headband that influences behaviour
▫️ A new China-US race in brain technology; the US is also reviewing a plan to restrict investment in Chinese companies in this field

🔍 Ditoman's view
The brain is becoming the next technology platform. The weight-loss drug market is one of the largest health markets in the world, and whoever finds a cheaper, drug-free alternative will change the game. But big claims need big evidence: until a clinical trial is published, this is an attractive promise, not a treatment. We follow this path together.
