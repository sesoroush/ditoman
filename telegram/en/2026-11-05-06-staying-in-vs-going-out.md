📱 Rising prices keep us at home; so why don't we invite our friends over?

In the Financial Times, John Burn-Murdoch has examined the common explanation for declining socialising: going out has become expensive, and digital entertainment is readily available.

💸 The rising cost is real
Since 2000, the cost of eating and drinking out and of events in the US and the UK has risen about 20% more than general inflation (an index of about 120, with a base of 100). Over the same period, the "staying-in basket" (groceries, TV, games, internet, devices) has fallen to about 74.

🏠 But get-togethers have not moved into the home
Seeing friends at home and in free spaces has fallen even more: from 2003 to 2025, by about 17 days a year, compared with about 8 days in paid venues. According to the article, Americans spent about three hours a week with friends in these places in 2013, and that has now roughly halved.

🏋️ We go out, but alone
Solo exercise has risen by about 15 days a year, and exercise with friends has fallen by about 3 days; the same goes for the gym. According to him, almost all of the increase is solo.

📊 Digital entertainment has not necessarily become cheaper either
The real price of "screen time" has fallen about 16%, and the quality-adjusted price about 39%; part of the drop in the index reflects better quality. Real spending per person on it, however, has risen by about 50%.

🧠 So what has changed?
The author's reading: convenience and the capture of attention. Socialising takes coordination; a phone entertains you instantly. This evidence does not rule out the role of rising prices, but it shows that the decline in human connection cannot be explained by money alone.

📌 Other data say the same: in the US, the share of people who see someone in person on a typical day fell from 38% (2015) to 30% (2025); in Canada, from 48% (1986) to 19% (2022).

🇮🇷 And Iran?
We do not have precise statistics on how Iranians' social habits have changed, and we do not want to make up a number. But one point is interesting: the article's logic says that when going out is expensive, home gatherings should take its place. In Iran, with high inflation, the home itself has become expensive: hosting means buying snacks, fruit and refreshments, and the prices of exactly these have risen. That is why many people feel home gatherings have become less frequent. This is an impression, not a statistical finding. There are also reports of fewer customers at cafés (from the café owners' union, unaudited), but busy cafés have been reported too.

🔍 Ditoman's view
Rising prices make going out heavier, the phone makes staying in easier, and habit locks in both. The good news is that meeting in person does not always cost money; a park and a simple cup of tea are enough.
