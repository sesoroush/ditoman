🔐 Cyberattacks on banks: this time through the back door of partner companies

Suppose your bank has strengthened the security of your account, but an attacker gets in through a company that builds software or provides services for that same bank. In the past week, four stories from four countries showed exactly this pattern.

⚠️ One important point: no evidence has been published that these are part of a coordinated global attack. Their timing and methods differ, and they should not all be explained under one label.

🇫🇷 France: about €100 million
▫️ Crédit Mutuel Arkéa confirmed a cyberattack on 4 November
▫️ According to the bank, the attackers got in through a service provider's platform and made unauthorised transactions from the bank's own account; customers' assets were not affected
▫️ On 5 November the bank said about €100 million was missing and that it could not yet say how much would be recovered
▫️ Blockchain analysts (including ZachXBT) claim part of the money was converted through exchanges into stablecoins and Ether; the bank has not confirmed this. According to these reports, Tether froze about $1.1 million in USDT

🇹🇼 Taiwan: 419 accounts
▫️ SinoPac detected unauthorised transfers on 3 November: 427 transactions, totalling about NT$41.76 million (about US$1.3 million), each of about NT$100,000 to an account at another bank
▫️ The bank says the attackers exploited weak security settings in its new mobile banking system, which had been supplied by an outside company
▫️ Customers' money was returned; no evidence of a data leak was found. The bank plans to seek damages from the supplier, and Taipei prosecutors are investigating

🇰🇷 South Korea: 40 incidents, 37 companies
▫️ According to Korean financial authorities (announced 5 November), 40 security incidents were recorded at 37 financial companies between 30 September and 3 November
▫️ About 473,000 "cases of information exposure" and about 1 billion won (about $745,000) in money taken
▫️ The companies: banks and savings banks, insurers, investment firms, payment firms, P2P lenders and digital asset providers
▫️ "473,000 cases" does not necessarily mean 473,000 people, and it is not "37 banks in one day"; it is a month of reports from different companies. The role of AI is still under review, not a firm conclusion

🇯🇵 Japan: up to 320,000 people
▫️ SMBC Consumer Finance detected unauthorised access to its loan guarantee system on 2 November and announced it on 5 November
▫️ The company guarantees card loans for partner banks and credit unions. Information on up to 320,118 people may have been in the data, including names, addresses, phone numbers, driver's licence numbers, workplaces and annual incomes
▫️ The system has been temporarily shut down and no secondary misuse has been confirmed. The partner banks have not been named and the exact scope is under investigation

🔹 Were these DDoS attacks?
Not in general. DDoS means knocking a service offline with a flood of requests. In France and Taiwan money was moved, and in Japan and part of the Korean cases data was leaked.

🔍 Ditoman's view
Behind a simple banking app is a network of banks, software companies, payment firms and suppliers. The security of your account does not depend only on your own password or the bank's systems; it also depends on the weakest link among its partners. The practical takeaway: get news about your account only through the bank's official channels, report any strange transaction quickly, and never give out your password, card details or SMS code on a call or in a message. If your data has leaked, the main risk is fraud using real information, not just account hacking.
