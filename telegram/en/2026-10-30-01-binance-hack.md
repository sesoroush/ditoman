🔴 Binance was hacked, but not the way the messages say

Since last night a message has been circulating in channels: "Binance, Bybit and Bitget were hacked, and $2.5 billion was stolen from Binance alone." Part of this news is true and part is not. Let's separate them together.

✅ What has been confirmed
• Binance, the world's largest exchange, has acknowledged that it was the victim of a major hack.
• On Thursday evening (GMT), a large volume of assets left one of Binance's hot wallets on the Ethereum network.
• Binance has temporarily halted all deposits and withdrawals to carry out a security review.
• Binance has said users' assets are safe and that it will cover all losses itself.
• About $2.5 billion of the assets that left has been frozen: Tether has frozen about $1.6 billion of USDT and Circle more than $800 million of USDC.

❌ What is not true
• "$2.5 billion was stolen" is not accurate. $2.5 billion is the amount frozen, not stolen.
• The real size of the theft is not yet known. Early estimates from on-chain analysts range from about $1 billion to more than $5 billion, and Binance has not yet given a final figure.
• Bybit and Bitget were not hacked today. The Bybit hack (about $1.5 billion) dates from February 2025 and the Bitget hack (about $387 million) from September 2026. The message mixed up three separate events.
• No official source has yet said who the hacker is.

📉 Market reaction
Bitcoin fell below $78,000 and BNB, Binance's own token, dropped about 6 to 7 percent. More than $1 billion of leveraged trades were liquidated in the past 24 hours, most of them long positions.

🔍 Ditoman's view

On days like today, false news spreads faster than true news, and fear leads to bad decisions. Three points to go over together:

1. Check the source before you believe it. Official news comes from Binance's official account and reputable media, not from an unsourced screenshot.

2. Watch out for "compensation" scams. After every big hack, fake messages and websites appear saying "connect your wallet to receive compensation". Binance will never ask for your recovery phrase (seed phrase). Don't click any link.

3. An exchange is not a bank. When your assets are on an exchange, the exchange holds the key; if withdrawals are halted, all you can do is wait. For assets you are not trading right now, a personal wallet (where only you hold the key) is the safer option.

💡 Today's learning point: what are "hot wallets" and "cold wallets"?

Exchanges keep assets in two places:
• Hot wallet: always connected to the internet so users' daily withdrawals can be processed quickly. Convenient, but exposed to attack.
• Cold wallet: disconnected from the internet, where most assets are kept. Slow, but much safer.

Today's hack was from a hot wallet. The same logic applies to us too: the money you need every day goes in "hot", the savings you hold for years go in "cold".
