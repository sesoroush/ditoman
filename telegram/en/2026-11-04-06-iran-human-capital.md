🇮🇷 A hundred years, two fifty-year spans, one country
Iran in 1305, 1355 and 1405 (1926, 1976 and 2026), compared with its 15 neighbours and with the West

📍 1305 (1926)
Population 10 to 12 million. No census, no university. Fewer than 10% of people had had even primary schooling, and the country's entire education budget was $100,000. The neighbours were in the same place: Turkey 10.5% literate (1927 census), Kazakhs 6.9%; Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, the UAE and Oman had only traditional maktab schools and not a single university; Iraq and Afghanistan had no census. Only Russia (53%) was ahead. The West was not uniform either: Italy was 27% illiterate, and colonised Korea only 22% literate.

📍 1355 (1976)
Population 33.7 million, literacy 47.5% (men 59, women 35.5), 16 universities and about 154,000 students. The Literacy Corps had taught more than 3 million people to read and write. Iran had moved ahead of Pakistan (22% literacy), Afghanistan (18%), Saudi Arabia (2% of women literate in 1970) and Oman (no university until 1986); it was level with Kuwait and the UAE; it was still behind the Soviet republics (Armenia, Azerbaijan, Turkmenistan, Kazakhstan, Russia) and Iraq. In the West, 27% of US adults had higher education, Japan 11% and South Korea 7%; Korea started from a point similar to Iran's.

📍 1405 (2026)
Population about 86 to 92 million. Literacy 87.6% (1395/2016 census; ages 10 to 49: 94.7%). About 3.4 million university students, more than half of them women, more than 80,000 faculty members, about 100,000 doctoral students. Average years of adult schooling 10.8, equal to Italy and Qatar, higher than Turkey (9.0), Iraq (6.8) and Pakistan (4.3). Higher-education enrolment ratio 57 to 72%, on a par with European countries.

🔬 And in scientific output, where Iran has its biggest advantage:
Of the 16 countries (Iran + 15 neighbours), only Russia and Turkey are ahead. With nearly one million scientific papers, Iran ranks 15th to 20th in the world, ahead of Saudi Arabia (36), Pakistan (39), Iraq (50), the UAE (57), Qatar (70), Kazakhstan (71), Kuwait (79), Oman (81), Azerbaijan (90), Armenia (93), Bahrain (102), Afghanistan (136) and Turkmenistan (192). In the Nature Index 2026, Iran is 26th in the world; Turkey 29th, Saudi Arabia 31st, the UAE 41st, Pakistan 42nd. In 1357 (1978/79) Iran produced about a thousand papers a year and ranked 52nd; today it produces about 2% of all the world's science, which several sources describe as the fastest scientific growth in the world over the two decades from 2000 to 2019.

💡 And from an angle few people look at: science per dollar of the economy
With about 2,600 papers per billion dollars of GDP, Iran is first in the region: 2.5 times Armenia and Pakistan, more than 3 times Turkey and Russia, 7 times Saudi Arabia and 12 times the UAE. In other words, with an economy one-third the size of Turkey's, Iran produces almost as much science as Turkey; and Iran's science is written by Iranians, not by imported researchers.

🏗 From science to work: the human infrastructure is ready
▫️ 234,000 engineering and construction graduates a year, among the top 5 countries in the world, on a par with the US (UNESCO)
▫️ 31% of Iran's students are in engineering and technical fields, against more than 70% in the humanities in Saudi Arabia
▫️ 25 million employed and 10,115 knowledge-based companies at the end of 1404 (early 2026)
▫️ 14th in the world for patent filings (GII 2025)
▫️ 94,000 foreign students in Iran, mainly from Iraq and Afghanistan; Iran is itself a destination
▫️ Iranian doctors and engineers have clients from Oman to Germany; Iranian universities are "more technical" than universities in the Persian Gulf countries

This means a people of whom fewer than 10 percent had been to school in 1305 built, within a century, one of the largest bases of educated workers in the Middle East: not with more oil than Saudi Arabia, not with more people than Pakistan, but with schools, universities and teachers. Almost every foreign source we checked says the same.

Ditoman's note: this country's real capital is not the oil under the ground; it is the 3 million students and 25 million workers above it. A country that went from zero to 15th in world science in 100 years is also capable of closing the loop of "university → industry → wages". South Korea started on this from a point further back than Iran in 1355 (1976), and today 70% of its young people have a university degree. This path is not closed to Iran; its human infrastructure has already been built.
