🖨 A 3D printer with 9 tools: Bambu Lab unveils the H3C

On 3 November Bambu Lab launched the H3C printer, the first of its third generation, designed from the ground up for "tool changing". That means a single print can combine several different materials, from PLA to carbon-fibre nylon and soft TPU.

⚙️ What does it have?
🔹 The Swaptek system with 9 independent print tools; tool changes in under 6 seconds. Each tool is pre-loaded with filament and kept warm
🔹 An enclosed chamber heated up to 65 degrees and a nozzle up to 350 degrees; suitable for engineering materials such as ABS, nylon and PC
🔹 The AMS 3H unit for 9 filament spools in a sealed chamber with a dryer up to 85 degrees; with additional AMS units, up to 24 materials
🔹 Build volume 330×320×325 millimetres; speed up to 1,400 millimetres per second (company claim)
🔹 A separate extruder for soft TPU down to 75A

💰 Price
$2,499 (US, excluding tax, shipping included) or €2,499 including tax in Europe. The sources do not give an exact shipping date.

✅ Why is it interesting?
Multi-colour or multi-material printing usually means slow, wasteful filament changes. Here each material has its own tool, so more colours and materials become possible in a single part: a rigid section, a soft section and dissolvable supports, all in one print.

⚠️ But before the excitement
▫️ No full independent review has been published yet; the speed and quality figures come from the company itself
▫️ The price is higher than the company's regular printers (such as the P2S)
▫️ More complexity means more maintenance and troubleshooting; one reviewer has questioned the long-term effect of "keeping filament warm" on quality
▫️ It has Wi-Fi only, no Ethernet

🔍 Ditoman's view
3D printing is moving from "making prototypes" towards "making functional parts". A printer that switches between 9 materials without manual intervention could make a big difference for small workshops, repair shops and local manufacturers, if it keeps its promise in real-world tests.
