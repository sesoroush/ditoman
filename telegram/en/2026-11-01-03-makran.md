🌊 Southern Iran is changing: from Chabahar to Makran

Is Iran building a new gateway for trade with the world?
On Iran's southeastern coast, several large projects are moving forward at the same time: the development of Chabahar port, railways, industrial zones, new towns and maritime infrastructure.
The importance of these projects is not just construction. If this infrastructure is connected, it could change the routes of Iran's imports, exports and transit.

⚓️ 1. Chabahar: a gateway outside the Strait of Hormuz
Chabahar is Iran's only ocean port and sits on the coast of the Sea of Oman.
Today Shahid Rajaee port in Bandar Abbas handles about 85% of loading and unloading at Iran's ports. But ships travelling from the Indian Ocean to Bandar Abbas have to pass through the Strait of Hormuz.
Chabahar does not have this geographic constraint.
A second route can reduce the concentration of maritime trade and, in times of disruption or crisis, give more flexibility for importing essential goods, machinery and raw materials.

🚆 2. The Chabahar–Zahedan railway: connecting the ocean to Iran's rail network
Track-laying on the 628-kilometre Chabahar–Zahedan line has been completed, but welding, completion of facilities and other work are still under way.
4,000 billion tomans in funding has been allocated to continue the project, and the stated goal is to put it into operation by the end of 1405 (March 2027).
Once the line is complete, Chabahar will be connected to the national rail network, making it possible to move larger volumes of goods between the port, industrial centres and domestic markets.

📦 3. What changes for imports and exports?
🔹 Imports: essential goods, industrial equipment and raw materials can be unloaded directly at Chabahar and reach different parts of the country by rail or road.
🔹 Exports: products such as Kerman pistachios, dates, minerals and industrial goods can gain access to another export route on the Sea of Oman.
🔹 Transit: goods from other countries, including Afghanistan and the Central Asian countries, can be moved through Iran to open waters, creating income for the country from transport, warehousing and customs clearance services.
🔹 Cost and time: large-scale rail freight can lower the cost of moving heavy cargo; the actual saving, however, depends on tariffs, route, capacity and how long goods sit waiting.

🏗 4. Makran: a project beyond port-building
The Plan and Budget Organisation has estimated the funding needed to complete a set of Makran infrastructure projects at more than 89,000 billion tomans.
These projects include railways, roads, port facilities, fishing piers, electricity, an airport and urban services.
This figure is an estimate of funding needs and does not mean it has been fully allocated or paid.

🏙 5. The new city of Central Makran: building an economy next to the port
The plan for the new city of Central Makran has been approved and is to be developed in phases with private-sector participation.
The importance of this project lies in linking housing, jobs, industry and services.
A large port without people, a city, transport, warehouses and supporting industries cannot reach its full economic potential.
