⚛️ The quantum race | Why are countries entering the field already?

Imagine someone stores a hospital's encrypted communications today, hoping to be able to open them a few years from now. Today's medical records will still be sensitive then. Security agencies in the US, the UK and Europe assume that this "store now, decrypt later" is already happening. That is why governments and banks are changing how they protect data before quantum computers arrive.

📰 The latest news
On 26 October Intel announced it is buying the quantum software company Classiq; Bloomberg put the value of the deal at about $2.3 billion. Intel's aim is to become "full-stack": quantum chips plus software for designing programs. Expected completion: first half of 2027.

🧭 What is the race about? Three branches
🔹 Computing: solving problems ordinary computers cannot handle, such as simulating molecules for medicines, batteries and fertiliser
🔹 Communications: distributing encryption keys with photons, in a way that exposes any eavesdropping
🔹 Sensing: measuring very small changes; for example, finding an underground tunnel from tiny changes in gravity, or navigating without GPS

⚖️ Why is it put alongside AI and nuclear energy?
The similarity lies in "infrastructure". Just as chips and data centres power AI and the fuel chain powers nuclear energy, in quantum the sources of power are chipmaking, control and cooling equipment, software and security standards. This capability takes years to build, so the race has started before the product is ready. The technology is also dual-use, and since 2024 the US has restricted exports to several Chinese quantum entities.

🌍 Who is ahead?
▫️ US: the tech giants and a DARPA programme that wants to find out whether a commercially useful quantum computer can be built by 2033
▫️ China: quantum is the first "future industry" in the 2026–2030 five-year plan; strong in quantum communications (the Micius satellite and fibre networks)
▫️ Europe: a joint programme and a "Quantum Act" on the way; the UK has committed £2.5 billion over a decade
▫️ Japan, South Korea, India, Canada and Australia with national programmes
Total confirmed government funding worldwide: about $40.9 billion

🔐 The tangible effect today: digital security
In 2024 the US published post-quantum cryptography standards; these algorithms run on today's ordinary computers and phones. The central banks of Italy, France and Germany, together with SWIFT, have also tested post-quantum signatures in liquidity transfers between central banks in "Project Leap". The UK and Europe have set 2035 as the deadline for full migration.
For Bitcoin: its signatures are vulnerable in principle, but the risk is not immediate; a debate on migration is under way.

🔍 Ditoman's view
The quantum race is three races at once: building the machine (perhaps a decade away), securing the supply chain and talent (right now), and replacing encryption before the machine arrives (the deadline is official). There is a lot of hype; these three paths are real.
