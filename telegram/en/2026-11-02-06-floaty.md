🕊 A drone that rides the air instead of fighting it

Researchers at the Max Planck Institute and the University of Stuttgart have built a robot called Floaty that has no propellers and stays aloft on rising air currents, like a bird.

🔹 The robot weighs 340 grams and is about 23 centimetres long.
🔹 It controls itself in the air with four movable flaps.
🔹 It uses about 10 watts per kilogram; multi-rotor drones usually use 100 to 250 watts.

🔹 Where could this idea be useful?
• Industry: inspecting the inside of factory chimneys, where rising air currents exist naturally. This is the nearest application.
• Meteorology and research: steering weather balloon sensors as they descend, and perhaps controlling rockets on re-entry into the atmosphere.
• Monitoring and rescue: years ago NASA added 60 minutes to the flight time of a wind-powered drone by using warm air currents; useful for monitoring forest fires and for search and rescue.
• Agriculture: perhaps for monitoring large farms, but not for spraying, since that requires carrying heavy loads.
• Military: this field is also interested in flight powered by natural air energy. DARPA has set up a project aiming for 75% lower energy use in small drones.

🔹 Why is it not a revolution yet?
Floaty has flown only in a wind tunnel, with a vertical airflow of 8 to 11 metres per second, and it drifts off course when horizontal wind dominates. Its makers' next step is not to remove the motor entirely; it is to combine unpowered flight with propulsion.

🔍 Ditoman's view
The possible revolution may lie not in this robot itself but in its idea: a drone that eases off its motor wherever the air can help. The biggest limitation of drones is flight time, and we'll follow together how far this idea works outside the lab.
