🚨 The biggest theft in crypto history, this time from Binance

On Thursday night (29 October), Binance, the world's largest exchange, announced that about $6.3 billion had been stolen from its wallets, most of it in about 20 minutes. According to reports, this figure is about 4 times the previous record: the $1.5 billion Bybit hack in February 2025.

🔹 A comparison that makes it clearer
Binance's emergency fund (SAFU) is about $1 billion, meaning the loss is more than 6 times its size. That is why Binance announced it has separately set aside more than $6.3 billion for compensation.

🔹 So far
• Withdrawals and deposits were halted, and Binance has said it wants to bring them back soon
• About $3 billion has been frozen on-chain; much of it was blocked by Tether and Circle
• Binance says it will cover all losses and will publish a new "proof of reserves" report on Monday 2 November
• The initial cause was an infected laptop, and later Binance spoke of "additional points of intrusion"; it has not yet found evidence of a private key leak
• Bitcoin hit its lowest price in six weeks (about $76,000) and then settled

🔹 Prime suspect
Blockchain analytics firms (TRM Labs and Chainalysis) consider the North Korea-linked group TraderTraitor the likely culprit; the same group the FBI blamed for the Bybit hack. Binance has not yet officially named the attacker. According to Chainalysis, attacks linked to North Korea account for nearly 89% of losses from hacks in 2026.

🔍 Ditoman's view
This event makes one point clear: the more assets are concentrated in one place, the more attractive that place becomes to hackers. The blockchain itself had no problem; the weakness was in "custody", not in the "network".

One change that could come out of this:
• Exchanges will probably be pushed to show their proof of reserves and wallet security more transparently
• Users will also get used to keeping their own share in a personal wallet instead of entrusting everything to one place

💡 A simple rule: assets you don't need to trade every day shouldn't always stay on an exchange.
