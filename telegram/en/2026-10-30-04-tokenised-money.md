💳 Money is quietly moving onto the blockchain

Singapore's DBS Bank asked 422 finance chiefs at companies with annual revenue above $500 million, in 11 countries including the US, the UK, China and Germany, what they are doing with "tokenised money". The result: 88% are either using it or testing it. About two-thirds have actually put it to work across the whole company or in some of their branches.

🔹 Which tools?
• Stablecoins: 63%
• Tokenised bank deposits: 53%
• Tokenised money-market funds: 52%

🔹 Why?
Better liquidity management, faster cross-border payments and easier entry into new markets. The head of this area at DBS says the change has moved faster than they expected.

🔍 Ditoman's view: what is coming?

1. Regulation is arriving. On 21 October the US published the final rules for its stablecoin law (GENIUS), which takes effect on 28 Dey 1405 (18 January 2027). From that day, issuers of payment stablecoins in the US will need a licence, backing reserves and clear redemption rules. In other words, stablecoins are moving from the edge of crypto into the heart of the financial system.

2. Banks have stepped in themselves. This month HSBC launched its own Hong Kong dollar stablecoin on its payments app, for more than 3.3 million users. The competition is no longer just between Tether and Circle.

3. But excitement is not the same as numbers. The Financial Stability Board (FSB) has reported that the total value of stablecoins is about $300 billion, almost unchanged from a year ago. 99% of it is in dollars, and most of it is still used for trading in the crypto market. "Testing" is also different from "widespread use".

4. And one point we saw for ourselves today: in the Binance hack, Tether and Circle froze more than $2 billion of stablecoins within a few hours. That is good for recovering stolen money, but it means a stablecoin is money its issuer can stop. This is a fundamental difference from bitcoin.

💡 Learning point: how is a stablecoin different from a tokenised deposit?
• A stablecoin is issued by a company (such as Tether or Circle), is backed by that company's reserves and moves on public blockchains.
• A tokenised deposit is the same money you have in your bank account, just in token form; its issuer is the bank itself, and it usually stays inside the banking network.

Money is taking on a new shape. We follow this path together.
