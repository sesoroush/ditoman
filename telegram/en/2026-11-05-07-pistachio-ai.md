🌱 An Iranian eye on the pistachio line: from Kerman's orchards to the global market

Behind a handful of pistachios there is more than orchard work; there is water and soil management, processing, laboratories, machine-building and trade. Now AI engineering has joined this chain too.

🤖 What's new?
Nojan, a knowledge-based company (University of Tehran Science and Technology Park), has introduced a system for screening pistachios suspected of aflatoxin contamination. According to a report dated 4 Shahrivar 1405 (26 August 2026), it combines visible and ultraviolet imaging with deep learning; the manufacturer's stated imaging rate is 100 frames per second. The pistachios pass in front of cameras and sensors, and the selected nuts are pushed aside with compressed air. This work requires machine vision, circuit board design, mechanics and equipment maintenance.

⚠️ It is only screening
The machine separates out suspicious signs. Research from the University of Milan stresses that fluorescence is not definitive proof of contamination and that chemical testing is needed. That research is not an evaluation of Nojan's product, and we found no public independent test of this machine's performance.

🏆 Where Iran's pistachios stand (USDA, October 2026, 2025/26 season)
🔹 Production: US 716,676 tonnes, Iran 225,000 tonnes, Turkey 120,000 tonnes; world 1.13 million tonnes
🔹 Exports: US 370,119 tonnes, Iran 145,000 tonnes, Turkey 47,600 tonnes
Iran is the second-largest producer and second-largest exporter: about 20% of world production and 25% of world exports (in-shell equivalent).

🌍 Destinations (Iran Pistachio Association, Mehr 1404 to Shahrivar 1405, i.e. September 2025 to September 2026)
India 26,722 tonnes, Turkey 22,408, UAE 14,992, Iraq 12,495, Russia 9,128, China 6,427, European Union 6,536. Turkey and the UAE are also re-export markets, so the customs destination is not necessarily where the nuts are finally consumed.

📜 A hundred years
🔹 1924 to 1927: a Kerman trade report recorded pistachio exports to the US
🔹 1930s: production of about 2,000 tonnes; today 225,000 tonnes (roughly a hundredfold increase, for production only)
🔹 1969: the World Bank recorded exports of 6,388 tonnes, worth $8 million
🔹 2024/25 season about 202,000 tonnes; the following season about 146,000 tonnes (a 27.4% drop)

🧩 The big problems
Water and land subsidence, salinity of water and soil, climate and alternate bearing, quality from orchard to warehouse, and trade standards and costs. A sorting machine improves only one part of this chain.

🔍 Ditoman's view
Pistachios can be a point where Iranian know-how connects to a real market need: alongside the grower, there is room for water and soil specialists, laboratories, programmers, electrical engineers and machine builders. How much this technology reduces waste and raises profit and employment still needs independent evidence.
