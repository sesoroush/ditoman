📘 What if your money had an expiry date?

You may have seen a post saying governments' digital money could have an expiry date, or could only be spent in approved shops. Part of this is real and part is exaggerated. Let's separate them.

🏦 What is a CBDC?
A central bank digital currency (CBDC) is money issued directly by the central bank, i.e. a digital version of banknotes. It is different from the balance in your bank account, because that balance is your bank's liability. It is also completely different from bitcoin: bitcoin has no issuer, but a CBDC is issued and controlled by the state.

🌍 How far has it gone?
• 146 countries are exploring one, together making up more than 98% of the world economy
• 41 pilots are under way, the largest being China's digital yuan
• Only 3 countries have fully launched a CBDC: the Bahamas, Jamaica and Nigeria
• The digital euro will not launch before 2029
• The US banned work on a CBDC with an executive order in January 2025

⚙️ What does "programmable money" mean?
Money with rules written into it: for example, it can only be spent until a certain date, only in certain shops, or only for a purpose such as food or transport. The rule travels with the money itself.

⏳ Is the expiry date real?
Yes, but only in trials:
• Shenzhen, January 2021: 100,000 people each received 200 digital yuan as a gift. The money could only be spent for 10 days, could not be transferred, and any unspent amount was taken back.
• Chengdu, 2021: digital yuan that could only be used for the metro, buses and bicycles.
These were promotional schemes. No country has put an expiry date on people's everyday balances.

🇮🇷 Iran's digital rial
Its pilot started in Kish in 2023 and covers person-to-person transfers and payments to shops. No independent figures on its users have been published, and there is no date for a nationwide launch.

❓ Questions to ask
Whether a CBDC is good or bad depends on its design and its law:
• Privacy: who sees your payments?
• Cap: how much can you hold?
• Programmability: can conditions be put on everyday money?
• Offline: does it work without the internet?
• Cash: will banknotes remain?

💡 When you see an exciting claim about digital money, ask: is it a trial or the main system? Which country? And what does the law actually say?
