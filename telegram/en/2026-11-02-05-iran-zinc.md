💡 Built Under Pressure | What lies beneath our feet? Iran and its zinc treasure

Sometimes we don't know things about our own country that the world is already counting on. This is one of them.

🔹 What is zinc?
The world's fourth most-used metal, after iron, aluminium and copper. Most of it goes into galvanising, a coating that stops steel from rusting. It is used in everything from bridges, towers and car bodies to batteries, medicines, fertiliser and sunscreen. The US added it to its list of "critical minerals" in 2022, and the International Zinc Association forecasts that its use in batteries will grow more than 100-fold by 2030.

🔹 What does Iran have?
• More than 350 lead and zinc deposits and occurrences (Geoscience Frontiers, 2018)
• Angouran, Zanjan: part of its ore contains up to 40% zinc, a grade that is rare in the world
• Mehdiabad, Yazd: 394 million tonnes of ore with 4.2% zinc, 1.6% lead and 36 grams per tonne of silver; a "world-class" deposit (Ore Geology Reviews, 2019)
The zinc contained in Mehdiabad's ore is about 16.5 million tonnes. According to the USGS, Peru's total zinc reserves are 18 million tonnes and India's 10 million tonnes. One deposit in the heart of the desert, the size of an entire mining country's reserves.

🔹 What major work is under way?
• A contract worth about $1 billion signed in 1395 (2016) with an Iranian consortium; the first phase of the processing plant has been built for $300 million, and its managers call it the largest lead and zinc processing plant in the Middle East
• Final target: 800,000 tonnes of zinc concentrate and 80,000 tonnes of lead and silver concentrate a year; work for several decades
• The plant's water comes from 100 kilometres away, from Yazd's treated wastewater, not from the desert's aquifers
• First quarter of 1404 (spring 2025): more than 170,000 tonnes of concentrate; last winter, production ran ahead of plan
• Zanjan has 480,000 tonnes of zinc ingot capacity, half of which sits idle for lack of feedstock; Mehdiabad fills exactly this gap

🔹 The Korean lesson: the value is not in the rock, it is in the know-how
South Korea has no zinc mines. Yet Korea Zinc, which started in 1978 with 50,000 tonnes, is today the world's largest zinc smelter and extracts 18 other metals and products from the same concentrates, from silver to germanium. According to its engineers, the secret is simple: they don't throw waste away. A country without mines became a chokepoint in the world's critical metals supply chain through processing know-how.

🔍 Ditoman's view | Our suggestion to the younger generation
Mehdiabad and Angouran are run by large companies. But the upper rungs of the value ladder, from pharmaceutical zinc oxide and zinc fertiliser to recycling steel dust, zinc batteries and mining software, are a place for small teams with the right know-how.
• Pick one rung and become world-class at it
• Start with knowledge that is cheap to get but slow to build: metallurgy, chemistry and materials, electrochemistry, data
• Define your thesis with a factory, not just a professor
• Work to global standards from day one
• Look at waste as raw material
• Don't think about Iran; think from Iran
Iran holds about 7% of the world's mineral reserves with 1% of its population, yet its share of the zinc market is still 1%. This gap can be seen as a regret or as an opportunity. We choose the second.
The treasure is right here. The art is turning rock into value, and that art can be learned.
