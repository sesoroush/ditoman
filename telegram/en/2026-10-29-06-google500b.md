💥 Google lost $500 billion in a single day

Today shares in Alphabet, Google's parent company, plunged more than 12%, wiping about $500 billion off its market value. This is the largest drop in value in the company's history.

🔹 What happened?
• In its third-quarter financial report, Google acknowledged that "AI Mode" in search has hurt its traditional search business. Both revenue and profit came in below market expectations.
• The heavy cost of building AI infrastructure turned the company's cash flow negative.
• The same day, ServiceNow shares also fell about 25%, on the same worry: that AI will replace existing businesses.
• The Nasdaq fell more than 1.4%, and the wave reached crypto too. Bitcoin briefly dropped below $80,000.

🔍 Ditoman's view: why does this news matter?

For years we have heard that AI will change jobs and businesses. Today, for the first time, we saw that this change does not spare its own makers either.

For more than twenty years Google has made its main money from search and the ads alongside it. Now, when AI gives the answer directly, users no longer click on links and ads. In other words, to build the future, Google has to hurt its own past business model.

This lesson applies to every business, big or small: if AI can do what you do faster and cheaper, the market will not wait.

💡 Today's learning point: what does "market value" mean?

Market value (market cap) is the price of each share multiplied by the total number of shares. When we say Google lost $500 billion, it means this paper figure went down, not that $500 billion in cash left anyone's account.

The same concept exists in crypto, with one important difference:
• Google shares trade billions of dollars every day, so its market value figure is real and reliable
• A small token may show a market value in the millions, but if its liquidity (the real money in the trading pool) is low, you cannot sell even a small part of it at that price

So whenever you see a big "market value" figure, ask alongside it: what are its trading volume and liquidity?
