🫧 $2 trillion in one month: is the AI bubble bursting?

The value of the 100 largest companies in the Nasdaq index (Nasdaq 100) fell by more than $2 trillion in October. More than $1 trillion of that drop happened in a single day, Friday 30 October. Now the market is asking a simple question: when will all this money being spent on AI come back?

📉 What happened?
🔹 Intel: a fall of about 29%, its worst day since 1974
🔹 Alphabet: about $475 billion of value lost in one day, the biggest drop in its history
🔹 Amazon: negative free cash flow of $42.4 billion over 12 months
🔹 Apple: with lower infrastructure spending, it overtook Nvidia again to become the world's most valuable company

💸 What is the main worry?
JPMorgan estimates that global AI capital spending will reach $900 billion this year and $5.5 trillion by 2030. Part of this money is also financed with debt, and rising borrowing costs have made that route harder.

🐻 Why do some say it's a bubble?
These companies' cash is under pressure and the market has become dependent on a few giants. While the costs are certain, the timing of the return on investment is still unclear.

🐂 Why do some say it isn't?
The revenues are real: Google's cloud revenue more than doubled and AWS growth reached 39%. Microsoft also showed that its spending is gradually paying off. JPMorgan's John Bilton says growth estimates may have been overdone, but growth overall will not turn negative.

🔍 Ditoman's view
This may not be the end of AI; it may be the end of a period when just saying "AI" was enough. The internet did not disappear after the dot-com bubble burst either, but not every company survived.
There is also a lesson for us: when reading company news, let's not look only at profit and revenue. Free cash flow shows how much money is really left after all the costs.
