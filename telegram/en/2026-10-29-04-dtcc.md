🏛 Wall Street officially moves onto the blockchain

DTCC, the institution through which most US stock trades are settled and which holds more than $114 trillion in assets, launched its own tokenisation service on 6 Aban (28 October 2026).

🔹 What happened?
• Banks and brokers can now turn shares and bonds into tokens on a blockchain and convert them back again, with the same ownership rights as the original security
• The first 20 clients include JPMorgan, Citi, Fidelity and Citadel
• For now it runs on the Canton network and DTCC's private network; more networks in 2027

🔍 Ditoman's view: what does this news mean for us?

In tense times, and in an economy where inflation reduces the value of money every month, we Iranians always come back to the same question: where should I keep my savings? Dollars, gold, gold coins, Tether?

Today's news shows the world is moving towards a point where "ownership" itself becomes digital; a share in a company or a government bond moves like a token in a wallet.

But there is a subtle point: these tokens are only for member institutions and registered, verified wallets. In other words, they are digital, but not open. The owner of the network decides who gets in.

This difference matters for us:
• Tokens like these, or even Tether, follow the rules of an issuer or a country and can be frozen
• Networks like bitcoin do not ask for permission to enter

So the right question is not only "what should I buy that will hold its value?" It is also: "who holds the key to this asset?"

💡 A small tip for today: for every digital asset you hold, ask yourself once: could its issuer lock it if they wanted to?
