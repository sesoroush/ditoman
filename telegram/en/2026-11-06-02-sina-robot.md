🏥 Tomorrow's surgery, made in Iran: the "Sina" surgical robot, from the university to the operating room

An Iranian team has been working on surgical robotics since 2003, and now, according to its CEO, a human study of the first Sina robot has begun. It is interesting news; we just need to read it carefully.

⚙️ What is Sina?
🔹 A robotic surgery system with a surgeon's console and instrument arms for minimally invasive (laparoscopic) surgery
🔹 Made by Sina Robotics, a spin-off of Tehran University of Medical Sciences (2015) after about 15 years of research; the lab dates from 2003
🔹 The commercial version is presented under the name Sina Flex
🔹 According to the manufacturer, the surgeon's hand movements are scaled down and tremor is filtered out (the CEO has mentioned a 10-fold reduction in movement)

🌏 What is better documented
▫️ Around 2022: Antara (Indonesia's news agency) reported that at least 80 surgeons were trained on the Sina simulator at Hasan Sadikin (Bandung) and Sardjito (Yogyakarta) hospitals
▫️ 2023: according to the company, a remote surgery was performed over a distance of 500 kilometres in Indonesia
▫️ An Indonesian surgeon has cited the 5-millimetre instruments as one of the system's features

🧪 The human study: the manufacturer's claim
According to Mehr News Agency, quoting the CEO, since mid-Farvardin 1404 (early April 2025) eight gallbladder removal operations have been performed (patients aged 42 to 63), and the patients were discharged the same day or the next day.

⚠️ What is still unknown?
▫️ These figures were announced by the manufacturer itself; no peer-reviewed data on clinical outcomes has been published
▫️ 8 patients is a very small sample; it cannot prove safety or superiority over existing methods
▫️ We did not see official approval for clinical use on humans on the company's website
▫️ "Training surgeons" and "operating on patients" are two completely different stages

🔍 Ditoman's view
The most important part of the story is the path: a university, long-term research, domestic manufacturing, training, and now a first clinical step. If the data is published and independently reviewed, this could be a serious example of advanced Iranian technology; until then, hopeful but careful.
