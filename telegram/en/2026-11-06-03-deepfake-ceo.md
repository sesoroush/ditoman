📞 It was the boss's voice, but it wasn't him!

Imagine you work in a company's accounts department. An email arrives from the CEO: "A confidential deal is under way, and I need your help." Then he makes a video call; the voice is his usual voice, and you can even see his face. He tells you to get the details from the company's lawyer. The lawyer calls, explains things calmly and professionally, and gives you an account number. You wire the money.

Neither of those two people was real.

⚖️ What is the case?
On Thursday 5 November, federal prosecutors in New York (Southern District) announced an indictment of three people. The charge: over more than a year and a half, they targeted thousands of companies around the world and took about $129 million from them using this method. The charges are wire fraud, identity theft and money laundering; the case is still at the indictment stage and none of them has been convicted.

🎭 The scheme, step by step (according to the indictment)
1️⃣ An email from the "CEO" to a finance employee: a confidential transaction, no one must know.
2️⃣ A voice or video call in which the executive's voice, and sometimes image, was faked with AI.
3️⃣ A referral to a "lawyer at a major law firm" for confirmation; the lawyer was fake too. One of the defendants played the role of British lawyers and had a handwritten script for it titled "lawyer call".
4️⃣ Large wire transfers to accounts in various countries.
5️⃣ Converting the money into crypto to hide its trail, and splitting it among the members.

🏥 Who paid?
Court documents name two hospital groups in Missouri and California, a bank in Rhode Island, a fintech company in the UK, an investment fund company in Massachusetts and even a pet food company in Florida. In other words, the victims were not necessarily small, inexperienced companies; banks and hospitals were fooled too.

🕵️ The defendants
Three men aged 41, 42 and 54, with nicknames such as "Rocky Balboa" and "Superman Cash", from Spain, the UK, Israel and the UAE. Two were arrested in Spain and one in the UK between June and September, and the US has requested their extradition. One of them was also convicted of a similar crime in New York in 2008.

🔍 Ditoman's view
In this case no password was hacked and no system was broken; what was hacked was "trust". Until yesterday, a familiar voice was the best proof of identity; from today it is only a "sign", not proof. A few seconds of recorded voice from a video or voice message is enough to clone it.
And one more point: the fact that stolen money was converted into crypto does not make crypto criminal; that is exactly why all of us should know that blockchain transactions can be traced, and how these networks operate.

🛡️ Three simple rules to set for yourself and your family starting today
▫️ Urgency + secrecy = red flag. Any request for money that says "right now" and "don't tell anyone" is suspicious first.
▫️ Hang up and call back yourself, using a number you already have, not the one given on that call.
▫️ Agree on a "family code word" that only family members know. If someone calls with your mother's or son's voice and asks for money, ask for the code word.
For companies: no wire transfer should go out on the strength of a single phone or video call alone; get a second confirmation through an independent channel.
