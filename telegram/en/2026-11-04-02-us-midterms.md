🇺🇸 A political earthquake in America: Democrats win even in Texas and South Carolina

The 2026 midterm elections became one of the most serious tests of Donald Trump's second term. NBC News has projected that the Democrats will take control of both chambers. CNN and CBS have also called the House of Representatives for them. Vote counting is still continuing in some races and the final make-up of Congress has not been determined.

🗳 What kind of election was it?
On Tuesday 3 November, Americans voted for all 435 seats in the House of Representatives, 35 Senate seats and the governors of 36 states. This election does not change the president, but it decides which party moves forward or blocks laws and budgets in Congress.
Before this vote, the Republicans controlled both chambers, and the Democrats needed a net gain of four seats in the Senate.

🔵 The seats that made headlines
🔹 Texas: James Talarico pulled ahead of Ken Paxton. Democrats had not won a Senate election in this state since 1988.
🔹 South Carolina: Annie Andrews defeated Darlene Graham. She is the first woman the state has sent to the Senate, and the state had not elected a Democratic senator this century.
🔹 Ohio: Sherrod Brown took this seat from Jon Husted.
🔹 North Carolina: Roy Cooper defeated Michael Whatley, the candidate backed by Trump.
🔹 Georgia and New Hampshire: Jon Ossoff and Chris Pappas held these two seats for the Democrats.
🔹 Georgia governor: Keisha Lance Bottoms won; according to Variety, the first Black woman governor in the US.

📉 What did voters say?
According to the AP voter survey:
• About 6 in 10 disapprove of Trump's performance.
• About 4 in 10 consider the economy the most important issue facing the country.
• About 6 in 10 see high prices as mainly the result of Trump's policies.
• About three-quarters are dissatisfied or angry about the state of the country.
• About 70% want Congress to exercise more oversight of the president.
Trump's supporters, however, do not see this as the end of the road for him; the president's party often loses seats in midterm elections.

⚖️ What changes now?
🔹 House of Representatives: it can make the administration's budgets and desired laws harder to pass and expand investigations into the administration.
🔹 Senate: if control is confirmed, approving Trump's nominees for officials and judges becomes harder.
🔹 Trump: he remains president, and executive powers and a large part of foreign policy remain in his hands.

🌍 Ditoman's view: what does it mean for the world and for us?
💵 Markets: in reaction to the early results, reports point to a fall in stocks and the dollar, and Bitcoin has not been able to benefit much from the weaker dollar. The first night's reaction does not necessarily indicate the trend ahead. More important is the effect of this result on the budget, inflation, interest rates and liquidity.
🇮🇷 Iran: Congress can increase oversight of military decisions and the defence budget. But it should not be concluded that this result will automatically lead to sanctions relief or an immediate change in US policy towards Iran.
🗓 2028: this result could also affect the next presidential race, both among Trump's possible successors and among new Democratic figures.

🔍 Summing up
Not all the votes have been counted yet, but the main question has changed: over the next two years, how far will the president's legislative power be limited?
