🇰🇷 24 times more power: South Korea hasn't just built a faster computer

A comparison: if all 8 billion people on Earth did one calculation per second, it would take about 2.4 years to match what this machine, on paper, does in one second.

On 2 November South Korea unveiled "Hangang", its sixth national supercomputer, at the KISTI institute. It is now in a trial phase, and official service begins in mid-December.

⚡️ Power and cost
🔹 8,496 graphics processors and 9,936 central processors
🔹 614 petaflops of theoretical performance; 24 times the previous generation
🔹 205 petabytes of storage; 10 times the previous generation
🔹 382.5 billion won; about $276 million

🏁 The road was not easy
The tender failed four times, because the AI boom pushed up GPU prices. The project fell about two years behind and the budget ceiling rose by 53%. In other words, the AI race has also affected the cost of national projects.

🧩 Who gets this capacity?
• About 55%: researchers at universities, institutes and in industry
• About 30%: strategic national projects such as K-Moonshot
• About 15%: small and medium-sized companies, along with technical advice from KISTI
That last part is interesting: a company that cannot afford to buy a supercomputer can still use one. The previous generation was used in 1,730 projects and produced 1,520 scientific papers.

🚗 What will it drive forward?
Car crash testing, engine design, weather forecasting, battery design and AI for scientific discovery.

💼 What about the job market?
We did not see a direct report on Hangang-related jobs. But in the global market, according to Kelly's salary guide, 90% of data centre operators see staff shortages as a serious obstacle, and 84% of employers report high demand for work with GPU clusters. These figures relate to the US.
In our reading, this kind of infrastructure highlights several career paths: HPC engineer, parallel programmer, AI infrastructure engineer, simulation specialist, and data centre power and cooling staff.

🔬 Next step
KISTI plans to connect this system in the first half of 2027 to IonQ's 100-qubit Tempo quantum computer. This connection has not yet been made, and its practical use still has to be tested.

⚠️ Two points for accuracy
• 614 petaflops is a theoretical figure. The TOP500 ranking uses measured performance, and that result has not come out yet; so a "top ten supercomputer" is, for now, the Korean government's prediction.
• The competition is also fierce: the US Solstice system is expected to have about 12 times as many GPUs as Hangang.

🔍 Ditoman's view
The number 24 is not the only thing to take from this news. Korea has shared out computing power like public infrastructure, like roads and electricity, so that small companies and students can reach it too.
For us, this is where the hope lies: in 2021, with Simorgh at Amirkabir University, about 0.56 petaflops and about $4.5 million, Iran showed that its engineers know this path. We may not be able to buy 8,000 GPUs tomorrow, but skills such as Linux, parallel processing and working with computing clusters can be learned starting today; and these skills are not limited by borders.
